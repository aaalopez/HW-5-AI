Ticket 101 — Customer order: Bulldog tee

From Tauhid Zaman · wants 1 Bulldog tee (white, size S) · linked to invoice #501

Expected What we think will happen

1. Who the boss should call first, and why

Inventory — the very first question is whether the shop even has this tee in stock right now. That one answer decides everything else: if it's on the shelf, this is a quick "yes, ready to go." If it's not, the ticket turns into a restock/vendor question instead.

2. Delegations we expect

  • Boss → Inventory — check real stock for this tee/size.
  • Boss (or Inventory) → Accounting — this ticket is already linked to an invoice (#501). Since the tee is expected to be out of stock, Accounting needs to look that invoice up directly, see which vendor it belongs to, and check whether the shop can actually afford to pay it right now.
  • Boss → Customer Service — once we know the real stock and vendor situation, write a draft reply back to the customer explaining what's going on (draft only — nothing gets sent automatically).
We do not expect Facilities to be involved — there's nothing about the lease or rent on this ticket.

3. MCP tools we expect to get used

  • get_inventory_status — real stock for the tee/size (Inventory)
  • get_invoice_status — look up invoice #501 directly by its id (Accounting)
  • get_vendor_shipping_status — once the vendor is known from the invoice, check if they're actually able to ship right now (Accounting or Inventory)
  • get_cash_balance — check if the shop can afford to pay that invoice (Accounting)
  • get_pricing — confirm the real price of the tee, in case Customer Service needs it for the draft (optional)

Actual What actually happened Needs approval

Who the boss called first

Inventory — matched our expectation exactly. Boss's first delegation confirmed CC-TEE-WHITE/S had 0 on hand before doing anything else.

Delegations that actually happened

  • Boss → Inventory — confirmed 0 on hand, vendor (Bulldog Print Co) has a 5-day lead time.
  • Boss → Accounting — looked up invoice #501 directly: open, $840, blocking shipment (run 1); after a human approved payment, a second run confirmed it's now paid and no longer blocking.
  • Boss → Customer Service — prepared an unsent draft for Tauhid Zaman.
Facilities was correctly not involved, exactly as expected. One thing we didn't predict: after the invoice was paid, Boss still flagged a second approval need — placing a brand-new restock purchase order, which this system has no tool to execute (only paying an existing invoice/lease is supported).

MCP tools actually used

  • get_inventory_status, list_vendors, get_vendor_shipping_status — Inventory
  • get_invoice_status, get_vendor_shipping_status, get_cash_balance — Accounting
  • list_tickets — Boss, to pull the real ticket record
Customer Service called no tool directly — it trusted the facts Inventory/Accounting had already verified, per the "trust facts you're given" prompt rule. get_pricing (listed as optional in Expected) wasn't needed.

Final status & cash impact

needs_human_approval — invoice #501 ($840.00) was paid by human approval, unblocking the vendor; a new restock purchase is still pending human approval (no tool exists to place it). Cash balance: $3,400.00 → $2,560.00 (−$840.00).

Ticket 102 — Rent notice

From Elm City Properties (landlord) · note says rent is due in 2 days · linked to lease #1

Expected What we think will happen

1. Who the boss should call first, and why

Facilities — this is fundamentally a lease question. Facilities owns checking the real rent amount and real due date against the shop's own calendar, rather than just trusting whatever the landlord's note claims.

2. Delegations we expect

  • Boss → Facilities — verify the real due date and rent amount for lease #1.
  • Facilities (or boss) → Accounting — only if Facilities confirms the rent is actually due soon, check the shop's real cash balance to see if paying it is affordable.
We do not expect Inventory or Customer Service to be involved — there's no product question here, and this ticket isn't customer-facing.

3. MCP tools we expect to get used

  • get_lease_due_status — real rent amount and days until due, measured against the shop's own "today" (Facilities)
  • get_cash_balance — check affordability once the real due date/amount is confirmed (Accounting)

Actual What actually happened Blocked

Who the boss called first

Facilities — matched our expectation exactly.

Delegations that actually happened

  • Boss → Facilities — verified lease #1: $2,400/month, due 2026-09-02, not yet overdue against the shop's date_today.
  • Boss → Accounting — checked cash balance ($2,560 before payment) and judged the rent affordable; after a human approved and paid it, a second run re-checked accounting.
Inventory and Customer Service were correctly not involved, exactly as expected.

MCP tools actually used

  • get_lease_due_status — Facilities
  • get_cash_balance — Accounting
  • list_tickets — Boss

Final status & cash impact — and a real limitation we found

The $2,400 rent payment for lease #1 was approved and paid once, correctly: cash balance $2,560.00 → $160.00 (−$2,400.00). But re-running the ticket afterward produced blocked, not a clean resolved state: Facilities and Accounting have no way to know this period's rent was already paid, because (unlike invoices, which have a status column) the leases table has no paid/settled field for get_lease_due_status to check. So the second run re-reported the same $2,400 as still due and correctly refused to double-pay with only $160 left. This is a genuine schema gap, not a payment that failed — documented here rather than silently worked around.

Reflection

Based on the real Problem 9 run — see the Ticket 101/102/103 tabs and the Cash tab on this page for the receipts.

How'd the agents actually do, per ticket?

101 — Bulldog tee: good

Inventory caught the stockout right away, accounting found invoice #501 and the real vendor block, customer service didn't promise a ship date it couldn't back up — see the full trace on the . After we paid the invoice, boss didn't just assume the order was done — it correctly said "now we need a new restock order" instead of hand-waving it. That's the right call. The real evidence this worked is the money actually moving: shows $3,400.00 → $2,560.00, the exact $840.00 invoice amount. Only knock: it burned some extra hops double-checking stuff before we tightened the prompts.

102 — Rent: mixed

First pass was great — real due date, real balance, right call to ask a human before paying (see ). We approved it and the confirms it actually happened: $2,560.00 → $160.00, exactly the $2,400.00 rent. Second pass (re-running the same ticket after we paid) is where it fell apart: it couldn't tell the $2,400 was already paid and flagged it as still owed — that's the blocked status you see on the Ticket 102 tab, not a failed payment. Not really the agents' fault — the lease table just has no "paid" flag for them to check. Good reasoning, bad data.

103 — Bulk discount: best one

One clean pass, no back-and-forth (see ). Correctly refused to invent a discount policy that doesn't exist (there isn't one in the data), quoted the real $58/unit price, flagged the 12-unit shortfall and restock as a human call. No payment needed, none made — the shows $0.00 impact for this ticket, which is exactly right given nothing was approved. Exactly right.

Tool-wise, we expected 5 (get_inventory_status, get_invoice_status, get_vendor_shipping_status, get_cash_balance, optionally get_pricing) and got 4 of those for real, plus list_vendors and list_tickets we hadn't called out — get_pricing never got used since nobody needed to quote a price here. Where Actual and Expected really diverge is the part we never planned for at all — a re-run. Would one agent with tools have been simpler?

Honestly, yeah — for these 3 tickets. None of them needed real specialization; there was never a case where two specialists had to double-check or disagree with each other. Boss mostly just played middleman passing messages back and forth, and that's exactly where we burned tool calls and tokens — see the for the confused double-payment check and the for the early "discount authority" search loop. One agent with all 9 tools would've been faster and cheaper here. The 5-agent split only earns its keep when you actually need separation of duties — like accounting never being allowed to approve its own payment (that boundary is exactly why every real payment on the has a human name attached, not an agent). For "look up some facts and summarize them," splitting into 5 agents was overhead this round.

3 new problems this team COULD solve with what we already built

  • Another vendor invoice goes overdue and blocks a different SKU — same exact pattern as ticket 101 (see : invoice #501 blocking Bulldog Print Co). Inventory + Accounting already handle this.
  • A second rent/lease notice comes in for a different space — same pattern as ticket 102's first pass (see ). Facilities + Accounting already verify real due dates and affordability correctly, as long as it's a first-time check.
  • A customer asks about a different item — stock, price, whether it's in — for any SKU in inventory/pricing (not just the tee or hoodie from /). Inventory + Customer Service already cover this with get_inventory_status/get_pricing, no new tooling needed.

3 new problems this team could NOT solve, and what's missing

  • Actually placing a new restock order with a vendor — the exact wall we hit on both and (Bulldog Print Co can ship, but nobody can tell it to). Needs a new place_vendor_order tool that writes a new row to invoices, gated behind human approval the same way execute_payment is. No new agent needed — Inventory or Accounting could hold it.
  • Rent across multiple months — the exact bug we hit on (see the too — the $2,400 payment is real, the "still owed" re-check is the bug). leases needs a paid-through date or a billing-period concept, plus a tool to check/update it. Facilities could use it as-is, no new agent.
  • Campus Customs opens a second storefront — everything right now (inventory, cash_accounts, vendors) assumes one shop. A second location needs a "location" column threaded through the schema and tools, and probably a new agent above Boss to arbitrate between two desks sharing (or not sharing) vendors and cash.
